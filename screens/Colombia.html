The Survival Guide

Culture Shocks

Navigating the beautiful complexities of Colombian daily life. Don't just visit—belong.

01

Hora Latina

Punctuality is flexible. "Ya casi llego" (I'm almost there) could mean anything from 5 minutes to half an hour. Relax and enjoy the flow.

schedule
02

Warm Greetings

Handshakes are formal; a light kiss on the right cheek or a vigorous hug is the standard among friends and acquaintances.

handshake
03

Personal Space

Colombians are tactile. Expect closer standing distances during conversation and plenty of friendly physical contact.

groups
04

Coffee Culture

Don't expect "Tinto" to be a fancy latte. It's small, black, and sweet—the social fuel of every street corner and office.

coffee
05

Safety: No dar papaya

"Don't give papaya"—a local proverb meaning don't make yourself an easy target. Stay aware, avoid flashy jewelry in crowded areas, and use common sense.

Cultural Insights

Culture Shock in Colombia

What foreigners commonly notice when adjusting to Colombian daily life.

schedule

Time & Pace of Life

Social gatherings often run on "tiempo colombiano" (starting 30-60 mins late). While work and school demand punctuality, social life moves at a warm, relationship-centered pace.

group

Warmth & Touch

Greetings are highly physical and respectful. Standard handshakes quickly evolve into warm abrazos (hugs) or light cheek kisses once acquainted, accompanied by formal titles.

handshake

Business & Hierarchy

Trust is built through initial small talk before diving into work. Decisions respect age and seniority, and communication is highly indirect to preserve social harmony.

public

Regional Differences

Colombia is culturally diverse. Bogotanos are more formal and reserved; Paisas show proud entrepreneurial drive; while Coastal regions reflect an animated, open Caribbean rhythm.

Advices and Experiences

See It In Action

Q1 - What was the most significant difference between your home culture and your new environment that surprised you the most?

If you hear people saying "Buenos días, mi reina," "hermosa," "bella," or "a la orden," don't be surprised. They're usually not flirting with you, it's just a warm, friendly way that locals greet and interact with others.

Q2 - Did you find that your perspective on your own culture changed because of your time abroad? If so, how?

Time just works differently in Colombia; schedules, appointments, and social plans don't always run exactly on time. Learning to adapt is simply part of the experience.

Q3 - Looking back, what is one thing you wish you had known before you left home that would have made the transition easier?

You should go and find out for yourself, Colombians will surprise you anyway.

Q4 - Could you describe the most challenging situation you faced, and how did you navigate it?

Even though Colombians have a reputation for being warm and friendly, that doesn't mean everyone is the same. Sometimes people's attitudes can change dramatically when they project their stereotypes or prejudices about your country onto you.
