USA 🇺🇸

Land of the free and the home of the brave. Discover the complexities, the giant portions, and the legendary hospitality of the United States.

Culture Shocks

Pro Tip: Be Prepared
payments

Tipping Culture

Expecting 18-25% at restaurants. It's not just a bonus; it's a fundamental part of the service economy.

restaurant

Large Portions

Meals are often oversized compared to Europe or Asia. "To-go" boxes are standard and expected.

forum

Small Talk

Expect "How are you?" from strangers. It's often a greeting, not an inquiry.

directions_car

Car Dependency

Outside major hubs like NYC, a car is essential for daily survival and travel.

work_history

Work-Life Balance

The "hustle" is real. Expect businesses to be open late and people to be busy.

Academic & Social Transitions

Culture Shock in the U.S.

How international students experience and adapt to life and higher education in the United States.

sentiment_dissatisfied

Emotional & Physical Symptoms

Following an initial "honeymoon phase," students often face intense homesickness, loneliness, and stress. This emotional tension frequently manifests physically as fatigue, headaches, or social withdrawal.

school

Academic Challenges

U.S. classrooms emphasize open participation, critical discussion, and direct communication. Students from lecture-based systems often feel insecure or silent due to fear of language mistakes.

forum

Surface-Level Relations

Initial interactions with domestic students are usually friendly but tend to remain superficial. Navigating unfamiliar jokes and cultural references can drive international students to isolate or seek comfort only with co-nationals.

psychology

Identity & Resilience

Students often negotiate an identity crisis, feeling like outsiders in both cultures. However, by balancing home connections, joining campus activities, and utilizing institutional support, they build lasting intercultural competence.
